# Aurelis go-to-market — first paid engagement

## Positioning

**Aurelis reconciles the full operating cost of an AI workflow with successful outcomes and customer-approved value evidence.**

Do not sell generic AI strategy, AI transformation or a dashboard. Sell a bounded economic decision for one live workflow.

## Competitive reality and the wedge to test

This is an existing category. Aurelis must not claim to invent AI cost-per-outcome measurement or to be the only platform connecting AI spend to business results.

- CloudZero publicly documents AI spend allocation, custom unit metrics, cost per AI outcome, shared-cost allocation and business-outcome attribution: https://www.cloudzero.com/solutions/ai/ and https://www.cloudzero.com/blog/cost-per-ai-outcome/
- CloudZero's unit-economics documentation accepts business metrics through telemetry/API or CSV: https://docs.cloudzero.com/docs/unit-economics
- The 2026 category includes FinOps, gateways, observability and outcome-attribution vendors; buyer tooling may already cover part or all of the need: https://infro.io/blog/ai-cost-management-tools-for-finance-teams

Therefore, Aurelis must qualify a specific gap rather than compete on generic dashboards:
1. Cost per *verified successful business outcome*, not tokens or requests alone.
2. Explicit inclusion of human review, escalation and rework where reliable records exist.
3. Source-to-result traceability, coverage reporting and transparent limitations.
4. A buyer-side decision memo that tests whether the incumbent's own data can support a scale/no-scale decision.

This wedge is a hypothesis to test, not a proven differentiation. If an incumbent platform already reconciles these items for the prospect, do not sell Aurelis the same job. Identify a distinct gap or disqualify the account.

## Ideal customer profile

Qualify only an organization that:
- already operates at least one material AI-enabled workflow in production or a controlled live environment;
- has recurring workflow volume and a named business owner;
- incurs identifiable model/API, cloud, tooling and human-review costs;
- cannot confidently explain cost per successful outcome or value realization;
- has an active decision to scale, redesign, pause or govern the workflow;
- can provide authorized, appropriately minimized exports.

### Buyer map

- Economic buyer: CFO, finance transformation leader, CIO or Chief AI Officer.
- Champion: AI platform/FinOps lead, automation director or workflow operations owner.
- Data owners: platform engineering, finance operations and workflow operations.
- Blockers: security, privacy, procurement and legal.

## Urgency triggers

Prioritize accounts where at least one is true:
1. AI spend exceeded its approved budget.
2. A new agent/workflow rollout requires a scale/no-scale decision.
3. Finance has challenged the savings or productivity claim.
4. Human review and exception handling are eroding expected benefits.
5. Leadership needs to allocate AI budget across competing workflows.
6. A model/vendor change has made prior unit economics unreliable.

Do not qualify an account based on AI interest alone.

## First offer: AI Workflow Economics Assessment

**Initial test price: USD 1,500 fixed fee. This is a proposed price to test, not a proven market-clearing price or a claim of existing customer demand.**

Scope:
- One AI-enabled workflow.
- One agreed reporting period, normally 30 days.
- Up to three customer-authorized data exports, subject to review before acceptance.
- One outcome definition and one customer-approved value evidence rule.
- Up to 10 hours of analyst-led mapping, reconciliation and reporting.
- Delivery within 10 business days after complete, usable inputs are received.

Deliverables:
1. Source inventory and coverage matrix.
2. Cost reconciliation by model, compute, tooling, human review and rework where records permit.
3. Outcome reconciliation: total, successful, failed, first-pass and rework where records permit.
4. Fully loaded cost per successful outcome.
5. Verified, estimated and unverified value separated.
6. Data gaps, assumptions, exclusions and confidence limitations.
7. Decision memo: scale, redesign, pause or collect better evidence.
8. Reproducible calculation workbook/CSV and management readout.

Acceptance criteria:
- Every included total traces to a supplied source record or documented transformation.
- Duplicate handling and exclusions are documented.
- Currency and period boundaries are consistent.
- Unsupported ROI is withheld.
- Missing data is reported as missing, not imputed as zero.
- Buyer receives the agreed files and a walkthrough.

Out of scope:
- Production integrations or write access.
- Changes to customer systems.
- Independent audit, assurance opinion, legal/accounting advice or certification.
- Guaranteed savings, recovered revenue or positive ROI.
- Handling credentials, prompts, personal data or confidential records without approved safeguards and written authorization.

## Complimentary 60-minute review

Purpose: qualify the workflow and agree whether a paid assessment is feasible.

Included:
- identify one workflow and its owner;
- clarify the business decision and current measurement gap;
- map likely cost/outcome sources at a high level;
- identify whether the buyer can authorize suitable exports;
- decide whether a paid assessment has a clear scope.

Not included: data processing, a custom analysis, a written report, a savings estimate or a free pilot.

## Recurring offer — only after proof

After a successful assessment, propose a monthly measurement service only if the buyer has:
- recurring source data and a repeatable refresh process;
- an ongoing workflow owner and budget owner;
- a monthly decision or operating review that uses the output;
- an agreed data/security path.

Possible recurring scope: monthly reconciliation, cost/outcome trend, exception review, source coverage, model/vendor comparison and portfolio decision log. Price only after the pilot establishes workload and willingness to pay.

## Qualification scorecard

A prospect is qualified only when all critical fields are known:

| Field | Required evidence |
|---|---|
| Live workflow | Named workflow, volume and owner |
| Economic pain | Budget overrun, disputed value, hidden labor or scale decision |
| Measurement gap | Specific cost/outcome/value records that do not reconcile |
| Authority | Identified buyer and budget owner |
| Data access | Authorized export path and source owners |
| Timing | Active decision or deadline |
| Paid intent | Buyer discusses paid scope, fee and procurement path |

A meeting, compliment, download or request for a free analysis is not a sale.

## Stop conditions

Decline or defer when:
- no live workflow exists;
- no accountable owner or economic buyer exists;
- the buyer cannot authorize data access;
- there is no material decision or economic consequence;
- required records cannot support the requested conclusion;
- the buyer expects guaranteed savings or unsupported certification;
- security/privacy requirements cannot be met.

## Commercial funnel metrics

Track weekly:
- qualified accounts researched;
- relevant buyer conversations;
- reviews completed;
- paid scopes proposed;
- paid assessments accepted;
- cash actually received;
- delivery hours and realized hourly revenue;
- repeat engagements and monthly recurring revenue.

Do not count pipeline value as revenue. Revenue means money received for completed, accepted work.
